SAR – Six Axis Robot Load Simulator

Side view · gravity torque per joint · default pose = fully stretched, horizontal

Side view

0° = link continues straight horizontally. Positive angle = rotate upward. J1, J4, J6 are roll/yaw axes: they carry no gravity torque when the load lies on the axis.

Speed simulation

Torque-limited dynamics at 5 ms steps. Speed is capped by the commanded max speed, by the controller step rate (motor rpm = step rate × 60 ÷ (200 × microsteps)), and by the stepper torque curve against gravity; the table shows which one limits each joint. Each joint accelerates as hard as the stepper allows at its current rpm (torque falls with speed along the curve set by corner/max rpm), against gravity at the current pose and the inertia of everything beyond it plus the reflected rotor (× n²). Inertia uses point masses and estimated rotor inertia, so treat results as a first estimate. Utilization = required motor torque ÷ torque available at that speed. A = start pose (current sliders), B = target pose; J1/J4/J6 turn by the given relative angle.

Results

Max payload = largest TCP mass that keeps that joint at the target utilization in this pose (the lowest value limits the robot). Utilization = gravity load ÷ available output torque (motor × usable fraction × ratio × efficiency). Keep it below ~50 % for continuous holding with margin for acceleration.

Parts (edit values)

Drive train: stepper + cycloidal

Ratio 1:n is a free design choice. Output torque = holding torque × usable fraction × n × efficiency. Motors sit inside the links: Pos = where along its link the motor + drive mass sits (J3 upper arm, J4/J5 forearm, J6 wrist; 0 = near the joint before it). A motor near the shoulder barely loads J2. The right-angle stage (bevel/belt) feeds the pitch joints J2, J3, J5.
